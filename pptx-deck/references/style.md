Wording and visual rules for the deck. Apply them to every slide.

Headlines
- A full sentence that states the point ("Support tickets fell 31% after the
  new routing"), at most 12 words. Not a label ("Results", "Overview").
- No colon-subtitles, no question headlines unless the slide answers it at once.

Body text
- At most 5 bullets, each at most 14 words, each a complete thought. If a
  slide needs more, it is two slides or its detail belongs in the notes.
- Plain verbs and concrete nouns. Name the actual thing, number or person.
- Cut these habits: "leverage", "robust", "seamless", "cutting-edge",
  "game-changing", "unlock", "empower", "in today's fast-paced world", "it is
  important to note", three-item lists made only for rhythm, a closing line
  that restates the slide, and a bold lead-in on every bullet.
- No emoji as bullets or decoration. No rhetorical "not just X, but Y" frames.
- Use the same words for the same thing across slides; do not rotate synonyms.
- Numbers carry their unit and period ("12.4 s median, March"). Round to what
  the audience can use.

Visuals
- One idea, one main visual per slide. Prefer a real chart or the user's own
  image over decoration. Leave empty space; do not fill it.
- One accent colour plus ink on paper. Contrast must stay readable on a
  projector: body text at least 20 pt, headlines at least 28 pt.
- Align to one grid; the same margins on every slide. Do not centre long text.
- A table has at most 6 rows and 5 columns on a slide; show the rest in the notes
  or an appendix.
- Never stretch an image. Say what a chart shows in its headline, not in a caption.

Structure
- Open with the point of the whole deck, not an agenda. Skip the agenda slide
  unless the audience needs to navigate a long deck.
- Close with the decision or action asked of the audience, with owner and date
  if known.
- Speaker notes hold the explanation, sources and caveats, in the speaker's own
  words, first person, as it would be said aloud.
